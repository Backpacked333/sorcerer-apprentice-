# Project Summary

## The problem you solved and its relevance

When an experienced person leaves, the screen recording stays and the judgment does not. You can see the click. You cannot see why, where the limit was, which case was an exception, or when they would have stopped and asked someone. A new hire usually finds those rules by breaking them.

## What you built during the hackathon

Simon is the apprentice we built for that gap. It sits beside an expert on a small accounts-payable desk, watches the screen, and stays quiet while they type, read, or talk. At a natural pause it asks one short question about what is actually there: why this, where is the limit, when would you stop.

When the task ends, Simon asks only what it still does not know, then explains the process back. The expert corrects a sentence or says yes. That yes locks a Work Map you can click through. Every step shows the screen at that moment, the decision, and the reason in the expert's own words.

A new hire then works a case the expert never showed. The same map becomes a tutor that steps in before a wrong decision is saved, explains with that reasoning, and replays the expert's screen. The expert does not write a handbook. The new hire learns the guardrail before the costly click. The same map can later load as rules an agent follows, and the agent stops where the expert would.

## What works today and what’s impressive about your outcome

What works today is that path on a live link. A stranger can open a finished Work Map with no microphone, or run capture, confirmation, and teaching. Simon does not treat a transcript as knowledge. The map stays open until the expert's own words fill it and the expert agrees, and only then can it stop the next person in time.
